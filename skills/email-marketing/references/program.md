# Program planning rules

## Choose a first measurable result

| Situation | Starting flow | Useful result | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signups stall before product value | Activation | First report, published page, successful integration | Asking everyone to upgrade immediately |
| Visitors want information | Opt-in nurture | A relevant product action | Adding researched contacts to the list |
| B2B buyer and demonstrable use case | Reviewed outbound | Qualified conversation | Inventing buyer pain from a job title |
| Active paying users | Retention/education | Repeat value or adoption | Sending irrelevant onboarding reminders |

## Flow brief

Write: name; business objective; audience and exclusions; entry event and data source; re-entry policy; timezone; ordered message purposes; timing; current-state checks; exit events; frequency priority; owner; instrumentation; implementation status.

Every planned flow remains `planned` until its triggers and suppression actually run. If only one flow is implemented, the map must make that distinction visible.

## Capture without default discounts

Offer a resource, product sample, or useful diagnostic that solves an immediate part of the buyer's problem. Compare effort and audience relevance. A discount needs an actual pricing/margin rationale and authorization. Write why the person should subscribe, what arrives next, and how they can leave. Treat recording consent, delivery of a requested resource, and promotional enrollment as distinct concerns appropriate to the product and jurisdiction.

## Contact pressure

Choose an initial frequency hypothesis, for example at most one marketing message per recipient in 24 hours. Label it a starting policy, not a universal benchmark. Product-triggered help may take priority over a general broadcast. Promotional sends respect opt-out; essential account notifications retain their own rules. If a campaign is deferred, recheck its relevance before rescheduling. A reply to outbound pauses its entire sequence.

## Complete 30-day plan

Include preparation, first cohort, observation windows, a review, and one follow-up experiment. A calendar can have deliberate no-send days. Use education, genuine product evidence, and offers when relevant; never invent proof to fill a content bucket. Record who reviews copy and handles responses.
